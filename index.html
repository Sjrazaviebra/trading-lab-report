Why read this if you do not trade gold

This report is not a book of strategies. It is a report on method: what happens when you apply to your own ideas the discipline you demand of others.

Most strategy tests fail in a particular way: they cannot fail. Four loopholes are enough.

The criterion comes afterwards

It is chosen once the numbers have been seen. A criterion that arrives after the result can no longer reject anything.

The benchmark is zero

Rather than a real alternative — doing nothing, buy and hold, holding a constant position.

Costs come last

The toll is counted at the end, when on short time scales it alone decides the sign of the result.

The trials are not counted

The number of variants searched before finding “the right one” is never fixed before looking.

The result looks like a test. It is not one.

I wanted to know what survives when these loopholes are removed one by one. The answer is: nothing. And the value of the report lies in the how — each of the 21 rules was born of a moment when I nearly got it wrong, with the exact number that stopped me.

A protocol that has never killed anything proves nothing.

This one killed one hundred and ten ideas, two of which a threshold rewritten after the fact would have saved.

What follows, tab by tab: the protocol in eight principles (02), the 21 laws with the number that establishes each (03), the register of the 110 ideas (04), seven autopsies (05), my 22 errors and how each was caught (06), what remains and holds independently of all this (07), the chronicle (08) and the limitations, self-declared (09).

SECTION 02

The protocol

Eight principles, written before the first measurement and applied to each of the 110 ideas. Each one removes a specific loophole — one of those through which a test “that cannot fail” ends up passing. They are presented in the order in which they come into play: what is sealed, what is measured, what is refused publication.

1 · Seal before measuring

For each idea, a written file holds the specification, the criterion that would kill it, the thresholds and the mandatory controls — then a commit. The commit hash is the proof that nothing was chosen after the fact.

The measurement scripts read that hash back and refuse to run if it does not match.

2 · Two windows, and a reserve

Training from 2024-01-01 to 2025-06-30. Verification from 2025-07-01 to 2025-12-31. A third period is kept untouched as a reserve: read once, then declared burnt.

It returned −0.06. No further test is allowed on it.

3 · Exhaust what is free first

Before spending a read of the verification window, everything that can be decided without simulation is exhausted: counts, overlaps, agreement between modules, cost arithmetic.

Most ideas die there, at no cost.

4 · Placebos that destroy exactly what is claimed

Not “a” random control: the control that destroys precisely the property the idea claims to exploit, while preserving everything else.

An idea that claims to read direction is confronted with a random draw of direction, with duration and amplitude preserved — 16 to 200 draws depending on the case.

5 · A benchmark that exists

Never zero. For a trend follower on a rising asset, the benchmark is buy and hold. For a direction signal, it is the constant position.

And the benchmark must carry the same exposure: comparing a strategy invested 10% of the time with one invested permanently is not a comparison, it is a penalty by construction.

6 · Count the trials

The significance threshold is corrected for the number of cells actually searched, and that number is fixed before looking. A decision grid is limited to three values.

One unspecified sweep was once costed at 4 × 10²¹ possible combinations: at that count, no measurement could clear 10 σ — the idea was frozen before the first number.

7 · N_eff, not N

What counts is not the number of rows but the number of effectively independent observations. One set of 12,000 rows was worth 707.

Another: 8,818 instead of 30,643 — enough to move a test from 9.99 (pass) to 8.20 (rejection).

8 · A floor of 30

Below thirty observations, no statistic is published. It is replaced by a dash.

A number without enough observations behind it is not a weak number: it is an absent one.

The test of whether a protocol is real: has it ever stopped you from publishing something you wanted to publish?

This one did so twice in a single day, on two ideas that a threshold rewritten after the fact would have saved — the detail is in section 06, My 22 errors.

SECTION 03

The 21 laws

Each one was born of a measurement, not of a reading. None was written until a number had paid for it: the figure beneath the statement, in italics, is the one that fixed it. They fall into four families, which answer four questions.

5 – 10

16 – 21

On the benchmark — “compared with what?”

A strategy never wins in the absolute: it wins against something. The first four laws fix that something, and it is rarely zero.

1

For a trend follower on a rising asset, the benchmark is buy-and-hold, never zero.

Any strategy that “wins” on a trending asset must first prove that it beats doing nothing. Against zero, it wins; against inaction, it may lose — and the second comparison is the one that counts.

The measurement: buy-and-hold returned +$1,767 where the strategy returned +$984 — 80% more, with no rule at all.

2

A sign-based criterion anchors to the constant position, not to 50%.

On a drifting asset, 50% is not chance — chance is the drift. The zero point of a sign criterion is “always long”, and that is what has to be beaten.

The measurement: paired difference z = −2.16 — “always long” beat the criterion under test.

3

A filter is only worth something if the group it blocks is negative — not merely “worse”.

Sorting is not filtering. A filter that cleanly separates the good trades from the less good ones still removes money if the trades it discards were still winners.

The measurement: a filter that sorted twice as well still destroyed 30% of the result, because the blocked trades were still worth +$259.

4

The benchmark must be exposure-matched.

A strategy that holds a position only a fraction of the time can only be compared with a benchmark exposed for the same fraction of the time. Otherwise the gap measures exposure, not the idea.

The measurement: comparing 10% time in the market with 100% is a penalty by construction, not a comparison.

On what destroys — the added layers

Bounds, early exits, circuit breakers, caps: every layer laid over a strategy is supposed to protect it. The next six laws measure what it costs, and on what condition it does not.

5

Any layer that bounds a position amputates a trend follower, whichever way the criterion points.

The gain lives in a tail of positions that run very far: whatever cuts the tail cuts the gain. It does not matter that the bound is “smart” — it cuts exactly where the result is.

The measurement: cutting the 24% of positions that ran beyond 12 times daily volatility removed 64% of the net result (from $526 to $188).

6

A structural criterion escapes law 5 only if it is rare.

An exit “when the fast direction reverses” fired on 90% of positions: that is a cap under another name. A criterion that fires almost every time is a bound, however it is dressed up.

The measurement: +$1,125 saved on the small losers against −$1,601 lost on the big winners — $1.42 lost per dollar saved.

7

A layer that places no order can destroy as much as a losing module.

An exit rule does not trade; all it does is close. Yet it cost almost as much as a module that did place losing trades.

The measurement: an exit rule cost −$1,022; a module placing 162 losing trades cost −$1,090. Almost level — and one of the two does not trade.

8

A circuit breaker is an instrument of drawdown, not of return.

It lowers the drawdown. On return, without an edge it is neutral; with an edge, it destroys part of it. It is judged on what it does, not on what is hoped of it.

The measurement: it lowers the drawdown on 7 instruments out of 8.

9

A guardrail pushed until it changes the nature of the object invalidates the measurement.

A guardrail set too tight is no longer a guardrail: it is another strategy, and what is then measured is no longer what was supposedly under test.

The measurement: at 35% of days cut short, it is no longer a circuit breaker, it is a tight daily stop. The entire pass was invalidated.

10

No risk management replaces an edge.

This is the founding law: risk management protects an edge, it does not create one. All the caps in the world only slow the fall of an idea that has none.

The measurement: a control that enters at random, with the circuit breaker and every cap active, drained $1,900 from a $2,000 account.

On controls — “how do we know it isn't chance?”

A control is a version of the idea with what it claims stripped out. If it does as well as the idea, the idea claimed nothing. The next five laws say what a good control must destroy — and what a bad one lets through.

11

The control must destroy exactly what the idea claims.

A control that destroys something other than the claimed property lets the idea through — it tests nothing. The right control is the one that breaks precisely the mechanism the idea invokes.

The measurement: a badly targeted control returned 21.9 ± 6.3 where the observed value was 18 — the idea seemed to pass. The correct control, which destroyed the right property, killed it.

12

Any statistic built on a maximum is mostly diffusion.

Maximum excursion, highest high reached, best unrealised gain: these are maxima, and a maximum grows on its own with time. What grows without a signal does not prove a signal.

The measurement: a purely random draw already captured 86% of the observed maximum favourable excursion.

13

Any signed-displacement statistic on a drifting asset measures the drift.

If the asset rises, anything that counts rises counts the asset. Once the series is detrended, the statistic collapses and long positions go back to being a coin toss.

The measurement: detrended, the statistic fell by −88% and the share of long positions went from 70% to 53%. The asset had risen +59.9% over the period.

14

When a pre-registered criterion seems to fail, check the control before touching it.

Changing the criterion because it fails is to stop testing. The first question is whether the control is the right one — and often the right control makes the criterion fail anyway.

The measurement: the right control made all 12 cells fail anyway.

15

84% of random grids passed one of my criteria.

A criterion that a random grid clears four times out of five filters nothing at all. A threshold is calibrated on what chance achieves, not on what looks demanding.

The measurement: 84% of random grids cleared the criterion, measured over 20,000 draws.

On measuring instruments — “is the tool lying?”

Before believing a number, you have to know what the instrument that produced it really measures. The last six laws are the ones where the number described the tool, the broker or the noise — not the strategy.

16

On a net result, a rank estimator first measures the broker's fee regime.

You think you are measuring the strategy; you are measuring the spread. The toll alone, with no decision at all, already carries a structure that the estimator reads as a signal.

The measurement: the autocorrelation of the toll alone was +0.86.

17

Faced with a tail cost, the median is the instrument that hides it.

The median answered “no expensive slot out of 92” to a question whose answer lay in the tail. When the cost lives in the extremes, it is the high percentile that has to be looked at.

The measurement: median spread per hourly slot flat between 20 and 31 points; at 23:00, the 90th percentile was 90 and the 99th, 118.

18

A ratio whose denominator is not significantly different from zero is not a measurement.

It was a division by noise. Before reading a ratio, read its denominator — and the uncertainty that comes with it.

The measurement: a ratio of 7.20 seemed to clear a threshold of 3 — until its denominator: +0.0128 ± 0.0129, i.e. t = 0.99.

19

Measuring redundancy on positions rather than views inflates agreement.

Two ideas can hold the same position for different reasons; counting positions makes them look more alike than they are. Redundancy is measured on what each one says, not on what it holds.

The measurement: 0.70 against 0.60 — enough to cross the redundancy threshold, or not.

20

Any question about the state of a position is settled in full simulation, never by offline reconstruction.

Three successive leaks, all in favour of the idea under test: truncating a loss while keeping the position, charging an overnight gap to a flat position, and letting the measured object drift.

The measurement: measured error, a factor of 100.

21

A result that contradicts a fact already measured is an instrument signal, never a discovery.

Before celebrating a discovery, look for the flaw in the tool. The reflex was applied three times in one day; all three times, the tool was at fault.

The measurement: applied three times in one day; all three times, the flaw was in my tool.

The hardest rule — and it bears on the protocol itself

A single unwritten convention is enough to overturn a verdict already given.

Five verdicts out of five were overturned in a single round of adversarial review, each by a single convention that nobody had thought to write down: the phase of a time partition, the exact definition of a threshold, the size of a counting block.

This is the most uncomfortable finding of this work. A protocol can be rigorous and wrong, because rigour bears on what is written, and what decides is often what is not.

The countermeasure adopted

An independent reviewer who recomputes instead of rereading, and who crosses the language boundary: if the measurement is in Python, they redo it from the production code, and vice versa. A reviewer who rereads finds the written conventions; a reviewer who recomputes trips over the ones that are not.

The method cuts both ways. An independent re-implementation reproduced a series of signals 161 out of 161, bit for bit, which validated the harness. Elsewhere, the same method revealed that a reference figure used for weeks (162) contradicted the frozen exhibit, which contained 161.

Gold disguised as a signal

The trap: a control that erased the drift

A multi-scale rule seemed to carry real directional information: over 200 draws, its control never matched it. It passed.

It passed only because the control erased the drift. The asset had risen 59.9% over the period, and the rule went long 70% of the time. On the detrended series, the edge fell by 88% and the share of long positions dropped to 53% — a coin toss.

The threshold that demanded the impossible

The trap: a pre-registered criterion nearly out of reach — and the temptation to rewrite it

A volatility-compression trigger fires 59.4% of its signals at night, against 42.4% expected: a +40% bias, caused by the simple fact that a night-time lull looks like a compression. The fix exists, and it changes 41 to 47% of decisions.

The opening breakout that dies on costs, not on the signal

p* = (R + c) / ((n + 1) × R)

p* is the minimum hit rate needed to be profitable; R the risk per position, c the round-trip cost, n the target in multiples of the risk. With c = 0 and n = 10, we recover 1/11.

A 10R target does not require a 9.1% hit rate. It requires 10.5% once the spread is counted. No mainstream tool displays this correction.

It is the most useful formula in this entire body of work, and it holds for anyone: on short timescales, this one correction decides the sign of the result.

The tool errors

Four times, the error was not in the reasoning but in the instrument — a function, a rule, an encoding, a control. Measuring the gap is what revealed it.

This point concerns every MetaTrader developer. iATR is not Wilder's ATR: it is a simple moving average of the true ranges. Median gap 8.9%, 25% at the 90th percentile. Any threshold calibrated “in Wilder” with this function is calibrated on something else.

06 — iATR is not Wilder's ATR

MetaTrader 5's ATR function is a simple moving average of the true ranges, not Wilder's smoothing. I built thresholds for weeks believing I was working in Wilder.

What the measurement showed: median gap 8.9%, 25% at the 90th percentile.

07 — A loss floor taken for a percentage

A proprietary trading firm's rule is a fixed amount below the highest realised balance, never reset — not a percentage of floating equity.

The two refusals that cost

Twice in the same day, rewriting a threshold would have saved an idea. Twice, refused. Refusing in the morning to move a threshold when that worked against an idea ruled out accepting it in the afternoon when it worked for one. The error, here, would have been to give in.

The consistency of the rule is the rule. In the morning I had refused to move a threshold when it worked against an idea. I could not accept it in the afternoon because it worked for one.

SECTION 07

What holds up

None of the 110 ideas survived. What follows, however, depends on none of them: a break-even correction, a way of sizing positions, three technical facts and the real cost of a round trip. Each point holds for anyone, independently of the rest of this work.

1. The break-even correction

Mainstream tools compute the minimum hit rate of a fixed-target rule with no costs at all. Yet the round-trip cost belongs in the formula, and on short timeframes it changes the result on its own.

p* = (R + c) / ((n + 1) × R)

p* is the break-even hit rate; R the risk per position, c the round-trip cost (spread and fees), n the target expressed in multiples of the risk.

Target at 10 times the risk, no costs
9.1%

The figure the naive calculation returns: one win in eleven.

The same target, spread paid
10.5%

The rate actually required once the round-trip cost is counted. No mainstream tool shows this correction.

On short timeframes, this correction alone decides the sign of the result. One idea in this work died exactly there: a breakout of the first candle of the session, measured over 1,034 breakouts, succeeded 9.59% then 9.16% of the time over the two periods — above the no-cost hurdle both times, below the corrected hurdle both times. The signal was not at fault; the spread was.

2. Position sizing — the drawdown is an amount, not a percentage

A given strategy produces a drawdown of a fixed amount of money per unit of minimum size. That amount does not depend on the account; it is the account that varies around it.

The same module, on a small account
21%

The module's drawdown, relative to the capital of a small account.

The same module, account five times larger
4%

Same strategy, same minimum size, same drawdown in money. Only the denominator changed.

Two consequences nobody shows:

The two constraints do not bite on the same strategies

The daily constraint bites on fast strategies. The overall constraint bites on slow ones, whose median daily drawdown is small but accumulates over weeks. Both must be computed, and the minimum taken.

A position-size calculator must be able to answer “no”

At the minimum size the broker imposes, with a reasonable safety margin, some strategies require an account several times larger than the user's. A calculator that always returns a number is lying.

3. Three technical facts, independent of any strategy

These three points depend on no idea tested. They concern the measuring instrument itself; the first applies to every MetaTrader developer.

  • The order book is not a matter of subscription. Out of 139,440 calls in simulation, none returned a non-empty book: the data is archived nowhere, so it cannot be studied after the fact.
  • More than half of order-book events move no price at all. A tick feed does not see them; any order-book study rebuilt from ticks works on the visible half and ignores the other.
